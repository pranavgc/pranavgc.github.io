Background

Building models,
and measuring them

I like questions that only a well-designed experiment can settle. Can a diffusion model tell you where it's unsure about a chest X-ray? Can a graph network tell eight kinds of particle apart from the showers they leave in a detector? Building the model is the fun first half. The second half is finding out how far its answer can be trusted, because one good run proves very little.

My first research Master's, in physical sciences at IISER Kolkata, was particle identification for the High-Granularity Calorimeter of the CMS experiment at CERN. I built the graph construction that turns calorimeter hits into something a network can read, wiring each fired cell to its neighbours in the same layer and the layers either side, and trained dense, residual, convolutional and attention models on a multi-GPU (Tesla P100) cluster. This year I reopened it for publication. On a rebuilt benchmark of 158,400 distinct events, a graph attention network reaches 60.7% across eight species and 97.5% across four classes, ahead of a CNN sixty times its size, and an arXiv preprint is in preparation.

My second, an MSc in Data Science at the University of Birmingham, asked whether a diffusion model's own sampling noise can say what it doesn't know about one specific image. Each chest radiograph is inverted to a fixed latent with DDIM and regenerated twenty times, so the per-pixel variance belongs to that patient's scan. Four of five pathologies localise above chance. Cardiomegaly sits below it at 0.450 pixel AUROC, which makes sense once you remember it's a judgement about heart size, something no local texture can point to. Halving the sampler's stochasticity removed 47% of the variance and slightly improved localisation, so most of the raw signal carried no spatial information. A follow-up with a LoRA-adapted generator, now also a paper in preparation, tested whether the effect was just a domain gap, and the answer was no.

I also like seeing models used. At Armour Group I designed the evaluation framework and confidence engine for a clinical form-filling system that is now deployed for testing. In two hackathons I built a reinforcement learning agent for DeFi liquidity, which placed second in its group, and a five-agent LLM football team on AWS.

I'm looking for full-time work in machine learning, research engineering, data science or quantitative research, anywhere careful measurement matters: systematic investment, applied research, generative models. Physics and machine learning are both fields I plan to keep working in, and the habits from one keep turning out useful in the other.

Jun – Sep 2026 MSc Thesis Deep Learning Medical Imaging

Quantifying Epistemic Uncertainty in Diffusion Models for Chest Radiography

My MSc dissertation at the University of Birmingham, done solo on two 16 GB T4 GPUs. The question: can a diffusion model's own sampling noise show which parts of a regenerated chest X-ray it got right? Each real radiograph is inverted to a fixed latent with DDIM (η = 0) and rebuilt 20 times under stochastic sampling, so the variance map describes that one image. Against radiologist boxes on VinDr-CXR, four of five pathologies localise above chance, led by Consolidation at 0.694 pixel AUROC. Cardiomegaly comes in at 0.450, below chance, because spotting it means judging the size of the heart.

Two cheap interventions showed where the signal lives. Cutting denoising steps from 30 to 15 halved the sampler passes behind a 39.6 s-per-image generation and removed 32.4% of the variance for +0.016 AUROC. Halving η removed 47% for +0.037. An N = 10 negative control, which changed only the estimator's sample count, came back null on both, which puts the effect in the sampling process itself. I benchmarked the maps against MC-Dropout logit variance and Grad-CAM under dropout, using Friedman tests, BH-corrected Wilcoxon tests and rank-biserial effect sizes.

Sep 2026 Generative AI Deep Learning Medical Imaging

LoRA Domain Adaptation of a Chest X-ray Diffusion Model

A follow-up I ran after submitting the thesis, set up as a pre-registered intervention. The thesis had traced weak localisation to edge dominance, with variance settling on ribs and the diaphragm, and it used RoentGen-v2 zero-shot. That left one obvious question open: was the generator simply out of domain? So I adapted it. LoRA on the diffusion UNet (rank 8, 1.66M of 867M parameters trainable), 2,000 in-domain radiographs, and captions stripped of class names so no label supervision could sneak in.

Edge dominance went up. Spearman ρ against a Sobel edge map rose by 0.046 for Cardiomegaly and 0.061 for Consolidation across 150 prompt-matched pairs (both p < 0.0001), and peak variance stayed outside every annotated box, 0 of 150 in both arms. A slope fitted before the intervention existed predicted the wrong sign for Cardiomegaly, so the observational relationship didn't survive the intervention. The five-seed version is now being written up as a paper, and full fine-tuning is in progress.

LoRA r=8 · 0.19% of UNet RoentGen-v2 · diffusers Pre-registered calibration test Sobel edge correlation 150 prompt-matched pairs

Jun 2026 Reinforcement Learning Quantitative Finance

AMM Liquidity Optimisation: RL for Concentrated Liquidity Provision

Forty-eight hours, three of us, and TurinTech AI's SaiFE Sandbox challenge at Imperial College London's UKFinnovator competition. We took second place in our challenge group. The task was to decide where to place a liquidity range on an automated market maker so fee income beats gas costs and adverse selection. Before modelling anything I decomposed PnL across 300 trajectories. In-range fees came to roughly 26 against about 4 of adverse selection, which made range coverage the real target.

A simple rule-based policy set the bar at +8.65% on 300 of 300 paths, with 31 rebalances an episode. Our first PPO agent scored +24.4 and looked respectable until we lined it up against the do-nothing baseline and it matched on all twelve metrics: it had learned to hold. A dense in-range reward, an anti-HODL penalty and entropy at 0.05 lifted it to +55.8. A three-phase gas curriculum (cost 0, then 1.5, then 3.0, over about 5.4M timesteps) produced the final agent at +48.02% against +2.44% for the best baseline, 19.7× better, profitable on all 300 evaluation paths at Sharpe 3.87 with a worst path of +12.9%. Evaluation ran at full gas with shaping switched off throughout.

The behaviour surprised us. The winner rebalances 291 times an episode and spends 87% of its stake on gas, yet each rebalance earns 5.30 in fees against 2.99 of gas, so we rewrote our opening thesis to fit the data. It shipped as a dependency-free NumPy forward pass under a 64 KB single-file limit: 4,931 parameters in 58.7 KiB, matching the PyTorch original to 1.02e-07.

Oct 2026 Agentic AI AWS LLM Evaluation

Agentic AI Football Team: Five LLM Agents in a Real-Time 5v5 Match

Five LLM agents playing real-time 5v5 football, built solo for the AWS Agentic Football Hackathon. Each player is its own agent on Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Runtime, deciding every 2 seconds, and the design runs at three speeds. A rule layer answers in about 1 ms, so every cycle has a valid action. Nova Pro then picks from a lettered menu of pre-checked legal moves inside a time box, which cut its output from about 40 tokens to 1 or 2 and made an invalid command impossible. A Claude Sonnet 4.6 strategist runs asynchronously, with at most two tool turns over MCP, and adapts the team plan mid-match. I picked Nova Pro with an evaluation harness that scored all six allowed models on latency and on a scenario exam with known correct actions: 18/18 at a p50 of 490 ms, against 13 to 15 for the smaller Nova models and about half the latency of the two Claude models. Forcing Sonnet into a single-field tool call with an enum-constrained schema took its valid-response rate from 0/10 to 10/10.

After that, the match logs drove every change. Replies of about 355 ms were halving the server's decision rate, to 19 to 31 rounds a match against 51 for rules alone. I moved inference off the critical path and mapped late answers by intent onto the next game state, so no stale command gets replayed; reply time fell to about 32 ms and decision rounds recovered to 60 to 68. The same logs showed the model's late defensive choices won the ball back 12% of the time, against 42% for the rules. Defence became a deterministic protocol (one presser, one passing-lane cutter, unique marking assignments) that every agent computes identically without sending a message, and the LLM kept the attacking decisions. All five agents reach the same plan, roles and reading of the coach's instructions from the shared game state alone, while the strategist publishes versioned plans to a single-writer DynamoDB store with freshness checks and hysteresis.

An opponent model classifies playing style from a 30-second window of features and switches among 9 playbooks and 6 formations, with confidence thresholds and hold times to stop it oscillating, logging each playbook's results to AgentCore Memory across matches. The coach's calls travel over a chat the opponent can read, so I designed a coded radio protocol whose one-time code words block replayed or spoofed calls and keep opponent messages from injecting into the agents' prompts. Under the hood are a synthetic match simulator, an 86-test offline suite, OpenTelemetry spans for each decision stage and a CloudWatch export pipeline that produced the per-decision datasets behind each iteration. One agentcore deploy (AWS CDK) creates the 5 runtimes, an MCP Gateway with 7 Lambda tools, AgentCore Memory, DynamoDB and least-privilege IAM, with model IDs checked against the competition allowlist.

Jaffle Demand Lab: Forecasting with Ground-Truth Model Evaluation

A forecasting lab where every accuracy claim can be checked against the truth. A seeded simulator generates 687k orders and 984k order lines over 1,096 days, so true demand is known in advance. I benchmarked nine models across 52,310 store, SKU and day rows with rolling-origin cross-validation, cutting RMSE 38.2% below baseline on a 90-day holdout that was scored exactly once. Predicting UK Restaurant Insolvency from Alternative Data

Can you spot a UK restaurant heading for insolvency before it happens? Most of the work went into the data. I resolved 5.6M+ Companies House records into a 24,750-sample dataset by matching businesses across four sources that share no key: Companies House, the Food Hygiene Rating Scheme, ONS and the Google Places API. Zero-shot sentiment from facebook/bart-large-mnli added a customer signal, and an XGBoost classifier (1,200 trees, on a CUDA A100) reached AUC 0.73, F1 0.24 and precision 0.48 on the minority class under 95:5 imbalance. Feature importance gave the useful answer: structural and administrative signals predicted failure better than customer reviews, and they're available before a restaurant even opens.

2021 – 2024 · Extended 2026 MS Thesis arXiv preprint in preparation Deep Learning Physics

Particle Identification in the CMS High-Granularity Calorimeter

My MS thesis at IISER Kolkata, which I continued for two years as a research appointment with the Saha Institute of Nuclear Physics, supervised by Dr Satyaki Bhattacharya and Dr Ritesh Kumar Singh. HGCAL is the silicon calorimeter replacing the CMS endcap, and it's fine-grained enough to record the shape of a particle shower as well as its energy. That shape is what a model can learn from. The task is to tell eight species apart at 100 GeV (muon, positron, gamma, π⁺, proton, K⁺, neutron, K⁰L) in a Geant4 model of a 40-layer, 12,502-cell detector. I built the graph construction, wiring each fired cell to its neighbours in the same layer and the layers on either side so the graph follows the detector's geometry, and trained dense, residual, graph-convolutional and graph-attention networks on a multi-GPU (Tesla P100) cluster. I presented it as a poster at the IISER Kolkata Department of Physical Sciences foundation day in March 2023.

This autumn I reopened the work for publication, starting with an audit. Five species had been simulated in parts without separate random seeds, so I de-duplicated the data, rebuilt the pipeline on 158,400 distinct events, and reran every network over five seeds with McNemar tests on the same events. A 46,920-parameter graph attention network reaches 60.7% in eight classes and 97.5% in four, 1.75 points ahead of a ResNet CNN with sixty times the parameters (p < 10−4), and the eight-class ceiling turns out to come from shower physics. Under standard training it dropped from 59% to 30% when noise-only hits rose from 27 to 78 per event, which I traced to stale normalisation statistics. Mixing four detector conditions into every training batch holds it between 60.5% and 59.1% up to 597 noise hits, at a cost of 0.27 points on clean data. An arXiv preprint is in preparation.

2023 · rebuilt Sep 2026 Numerical Methods Stochastic Processes Computational Physics

Numerical Methods from Scratch: ODEs, SDEs, Eigenvalue and Boundary-Value Solvers

Twenty computational physics problems from nine worksheets, rebuilt in 2026 with one rule: write every algorithm by hand. No SciPy, no numpy.linalg, no odeint or solve_ivp; the only imports are NumPy and Matplotlib. With no library call to hide behind, an integrator either hits its theoretical convergence order or visibly misses it. The suite covers RK4 and Verlet for ODEs, Numerov for the Schrödinger equation, Euler–Maruyama for SDEs including Black–Scholes, Box–Muller and inverse-transform sampling for Monte Carlo, modified Gram–Schmidt with QR iteration and power iteration with deflation for eigenvalues, and shooting methods driven by bisection, secant and a two-dimensional Newton solve.

Every script checks itself against a closed form. Measured convergence orders come out at 2.000 for Verlet, 3.976 for RK4 and 0.498 for Euler–Maruyama, against theoretical values of 2, 4 and 0.5. The Numerov ground state lands within 1.0e-11 of the analytic infinite-well energy, and the shooting solver recovers the exact initial slope to ten decimal places at an rms of 9.1e-13. Random-walk variance measures 1.0034N against an exact N, and a birth–death process settles at mean 49.85 and variance 49.06, the Poisson signature you'd hope to see. Histogram binning went from O(N²) to O(N), vectorising the random walk took it from 100 walkers in 8.9 s to 20,000 in 3.9 s, and the whole suite runs in about 59 seconds.

May – Oct 2026 LLM Evaluation Multimodal AI Clinical

Patients Over Paperwork: Confidence Scoring for Clinical Form Extraction

AI Developer Intern at Armour Group, one of three engineers on Patients Over Paperwork, a system that turns recorded medical consultations into completed clinical forms. The product supports 11 form types and 38+ languages and is deployed for testing. I designed its evaluation framework and confidence-scoring engine, and my evaluation work focused on English-language UK consultations. The guiding rule was to abstain before guessing: a blank field costs a clinician a few seconds of typing, while a wrong field marked as trustworthy is a patient-safety event on a form a prescriber signs. So the main metric was the false-green rate, the share of auto-filled fields that are wrong, and the auto-fill threshold is fitted to a target false-green rate. Five critical fields, from patient ID to prescribed product, always stop at the review tier.

Audio is transcribed with MAI-Transcribe-2, clinical terms are corrected, and GPT-6 Astra Pro extracts each field together with a verbatim supporting quote. Every quote is checked against the transcript before the field lands in auto-fill, review or manual entry. My confidence engine scores each field as a weighted mean of the signals present, weighted by how hard each one is to fake: evidence found and quote support count 0.25 each, the model's own confidence just 0.05. Four vetoes and three caps sit on top, and short quotes that recur in a transcript are discounted (a bare "yes" appeared 34 times in one recording). Confidently wrong auto-fills fell from 48% on the first evidence-checked run to 5.6% (1 of 18) at the strictest threshold on the final run, and the critical-field error rate fell from 0.71 to 0.24.

The largest single gain came from fixing the measurement. Of 9 high-confidence fields scored wrong, only 1 was a real clinical error; the others were wording variants, a number compared against a string, empty ground truth or a misaligned row. I found and fixed 12 defects in the metrics and engineering, including one that made the system look safer than it was: 30 g and 30 mg both normalised to 30.0, so a tenfold dose error scored as correct. I audited the ground truth (26 annotation defects across 42 forms) and built the batch harness, offline re-scoring with zero model calls, paired runs on cached inputs, and a threshold-fitting tool that applies the engine's own vetoes and caps. With 1 wrong in 18 carrying a 95% interval of roughly 0.1% to 27%, I recommended holding auto-fill back until more data supports a threshold. For handover I added an evaluation switch, per-request model choice (tested under 8 concurrent threads), a streaming FastAPI service and a Gradio dashboard for side-by-side model comparison.
