Background

Building models,
and measuring them

I build machine learning systems. Most of my effort goes into measuring whether they actually work, because any model looks good on the run you decided to keep. It's the second run that tells you something.

That comes out of two research Master's, both ending in a thesis. The first, in physical sciences at IISER Kolkata, was particle identification in the High-Granularity Calorimeter of the CMS experiment at CERN. I built the standalone Geant4 simulation that generated the training data — roughly 100,000 events across eight particle types — and the graph construction over calorimeter hits that fed the graph networks, wiring each fired cell to its six in-layer neighbours and the corresponding cell blocks in the layers either side. Dense, residual, convolutional and attention architectures trained on a multi-GPU (Tesla P100) cluster, reaching 94.76% ANN and 96.66% GNN accuracy.

My second Master's thesis, in data science at Birmingham, asked whether a diffusion model's own sampling noise can be read as an account of what it does and doesn't know about one specific image. A real chest radiograph is inverted to a fixed latent by DDIM inversion, then reconstructed twenty times under stochastic sampling, so the per-pixel variance belongs to that patient's image rather than to the generator in general. Scored against radiologist annotations across five pathologies, four localise above chance and one — Cardiomegaly, at 0.450 pixel AUROC — sits below it, because that finding is a judgement about heart size relative to the chest rather than a local texture anything can point at. Halving the sampler's stochasticity stripped 47% of the variance and left localisation slightly better, which says most of the raw signal carries no spatial information at all. I later LoRA fine-tuned the generator on in-domain data to test whether the whole effect was just a domain gap. It wasn't.

The thesis ended with a pre-registered five-seed replication that made me withdraw one of my own significant results. Training-seed variance turned out to exceed test-set variance.

I'm after machine learning and research engineering work where both halves count — systematic investment, applied research, generative models. Physics and machine learning are two fields I work in, not one I left for the other. Different problems, same question underneath: signal, or story?

Jun – Sep 2026 MSc Thesis Deep Learning Medical Imaging

Quantifying Epistemic Uncertainty in Diffusion Models for Chest Radiography

MSc dissertation, University of Birmingham — sole contributor across architecture, experiments and write-up, on 2 × T4 with 16 GB each. Asks whether a diffusion model's own sampling noise can say which parts of a generated chest X-ray it actually got right. Each real radiograph is inverted to a fixed latent by DDIM inversion (η = 0) and reconstructed 20 times under stochastic sampling, so the per-pixel variance belongs to that one image rather than to the generator in general. Scored on VinDr-CXR against radiologist bounding boxes across five pathologies: four localise above chance — Consolidation highest at 0.694 pixel AUROC — while Cardiomegaly sits below it at 0.450, because the finding is a global size judgement rather than a local texture. Cutting denoising steps from 30 to 15 halved the sampler passes behind a 39.6 s-per-image generation and removed 32.4% of the variance for +0.016 AUROC; halving η removed 47% for +0.037. An N = 10 negative control that varies only the estimator's sample count returned null on both, which places the effect in the sampling process rather than in the measurement. Benchmarked against MC-Dropout logit variance and Grad-CAM under dropout with Friedman tests, BH-corrected Wilcoxon and rank-biserial effect sizes.

Sep 2026 Generative AI Deep Learning Medical Imaging

LoRA Domain Adaptation of a Chest X-ray Diffusion Model

An independent follow-up to the MSc thesis, run after submission, and a pre-registered intervention rather than a post-hoc explanation. The thesis blamed weak pathology localisation on edge dominance — variance settling on ribs and diaphragm rather than lesions — but it used RoentGen-v2 zero-shot, which left the obvious objection untested: maybe the maps were edge-dominated because the generator was out of domain. So I adapted the generator. LoRA on the diffusion UNet, rank 8, 1.66M of 867M parameters trainable, 2,000 in-domain radiographs, captions stripped of class names so the weights could not smuggle in label supervision. Edge dominance rose rather than fell — Spearman ρ against a Sobel edge map up 0.046 on Cardiomegaly and 0.061 on Consolidation across 150 prompt-matched pairs, both p < 0.0001. A slope fitted before the intervention existed predicted Consolidation's AUROC shift to within 0.002 and got Cardiomegaly's sign wrong: an observational relationship that did not survive contact with an intervention. Peak variance still landed outside every annotated box, 0 of 150 in both arms. Full fine-tuning is in progress.

03
Sep 2026 Forecasting Data Engineering

Jaffle Demand Lab — Forecasting with Ground-Truth Model Evaluation

A forecasting system built so that every accuracy claim can be checked against ground truth rather than against another model's output. A seeded simulator generates 687k orders and 984k order lines across 1,096 days, which is what makes the ground truth exist in the first place. Nine models were benchmarked over 52,310 store × SKU × day rows under rolling-origin cross-validation, cutting RMSE 38.2% below baseline on a 90-day holdout scored exactly once. A Poisson fit was replaced with a Negative Binomial GLM after measuring overdispersion at Pearson χ²/df = 4.80. The result worth keeping is about validation rather than accuracy: random K-fold overstated performance by 6.1% against a size-matched rolling fold — measured, not assumed — with point-in-time correctness enforced by a dbt test that recomputes every rolling window. Five injected source faults each pair a detecting test with a repairing model, under CI on every commit, and the whole thing runs from a clean clone on DuckDB with no cloud credentials.

Jan – Mar 2026 ML Alternative Data

Predicting UK Restaurant Insolvency from Alternative Data

An early-warning system for the failure of food and beverage businesses across the UK. The hard part was the data, not the model: 5.6M+ Companies House records resolved into a 24,750-sample dataset by matching entities across four sources that share no primary key — Companies House, the Food Hygiene Rating Scheme, ONS and the Google Places API. Zero-shot sentiment classification with facebook/bart-large-mnli supplied the consumer-facing signal, and an XGBoost classifier (1,200 trees, CUDA A100) reached F1 = 0.24, precision = 0.48 and AUC = 0.73 on the minority class under 95:5 imbalance. Gain-based feature importance gave the finding worth keeping: structural and administrative signals beat customer-facing metrics as failure predictors, and they are available before a restaurant opens its doors.

May 2021 – Jun 2024 MS Thesis Deep Learning Physics

Particle Identification in the CMS High-Granularity Calorimeter

MS thesis in Physical Sciences at IISER Kolkata, continued as a two-year post-MS research appointment with the Saha Institute of Nuclear Physics, supervised by Dr Satyaki Bhattacharya and Dr Ritesh Kumar Singh. HGCAL is the silicon calorimeter replacing the CMS endcap; its granularity turns a particle shower into a five-dimensional object rather than an energy number, which is what makes shower shape learnable at all. I built the standalone Geant4 simulation behind the training data — roughly 100,000 events across eight particle types (muon, positron, gamma, π⁺, proton, K⁺, neutron, K⁰L) — and the graph construction over calorimeter hits, wiring each fired cell to its six in-layer neighbours and the corresponding cell blocks in the layers on either side, so the topology encodes detector geometry rather than a learned similarity. Dense, residual, graph-convolutional and graph-attention architectures were trained on a multi-GPU (Tesla P100) cluster, reaching 94.76% ANN and 96.66% GNN accuracy. Attention beat plain convolution across every feature set tested, residual connections helped both families, and adding simulated detector noise held accuracy up under noisier inputs. Presented as a poster at the IISER Kolkata Department of Physical Sciences foundation day, March 2023.

Geant4 · ~100k simulated events 8-particle classification GCN · GAT · Residual PyTorch Geometric Multi-GPU (Tesla P100) cluster CERN CMS Experiment

May – Sep 2026 Multimodal AI Clinical

Clinical Form Extraction from Multilingual Consultation Audio

AI Developer Intern at Armour Group. Took a hackathon prototype through to implementation: the extraction pipeline was merged into the product codebase and submitted for a clinical pilot, built alongside clinicians and engineers who set the review thresholds and the failure cases. The system is a six-stage multimodal pipeline turning multilingual consultation audio into structured clinical forms — 11+ form types, 38+ languages — to which I added a second-pass reviewer for fields lost at segmentation boundaries and an evidence-verification stage for every extracted value. Rebuilding the confidence engine around asymmetric clinical cost cut Brier score from 0.276 to 0.100 on the reference recording and raised high-confidence-tier precision to 0.93. The veto layer was measured rather than assumed: it removes 60% of extracted fields and 72% of the errors, a 67% hit rate against a 48% base error rate. Every field cites a verbatim transcript span scored for presence, entailment and uniqueness, so an ambiguous span such as a bare "yes" gets discounted. I also built the batch evaluation harness and a threshold-fitting tool sweeping false-auto-fill rate against coverage, plus transcription caching with deterministic sampling so evaluation runs repeat exactly.
